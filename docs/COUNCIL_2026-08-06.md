## 1. VERDICT

### Is it a Jarvis-level second operator?

**No. It is a superbly engineered advisor with an acting layer that has never been switched on,
and whose acting layer — when switched on — is fitted to two doors, both with a broken hinge.**

The Adversary's count is the one to hold onto: `ACTION_CLASSES` declares eleven action classes.
Six are inert (no finalizer, no production caller): `email.send`, `outreach.send`, `wealth.trade`,
`training.prescribe`, `content.draft`, `systems.sop_draft`. Two more — `research.ingest`,
`knowledge.apply_proposal` — only call `executeAction` from *inside* a granted branch
(`initiative.ts:114`, `proposals.ts:171`, `queueSweep.ts:65`), so the ungranted path never reaches
the executor at all; there is no gate to hold and no way to earn a grant. `content.publish` is
Cole-initiated and config-blocked. That leaves **two** classes where the gate is actually a gate:
`calendar.schedule_protection` and `inbox.triage_draft`. Both ship with an unchecked runtime
prerequisite. The first computes "opportunities" from an empty calendar mirror; the second writes
raw model text into Gmail with no honest-failure guard.

Verified live at time of writing: `select count(*) from "AutonomyGrant"` → **0**. The number of
autonomous actions this system performs today is zero.

### Does it double or triple his workload capacity?

**No. It is net negative today and roughly break-even at a disciplined steady state.** Every chair
independently modelled hours; the honest gross ceiling, fully fuelled and fully granted, is
**1.5–2.5 hrs/week removed**, against **2.5–4.5 hrs/week of review burden** plus a maintenance
floor set by ~39k lines of backend with one part-time owner.

`docs/SCOPE.md`'s "~5 hrs/week back" and "stands in for the load of a VA ($1–2k/mo)" are
unsupported by anything in the codebase. Nothing in `aurelius/measurement/` measures hours. Four
chairs flagged it independently. That document is the single most likely thing to make Cole feel
lied to in month two.

The Chief of Staff's revised framing is the sharpest version:

> "My prior verdict was 'net negative today, roughly break-even at a disciplined steady state.'
> Findings (1) and (2) are precisely the mechanisms that prevent the disciplined steady state
> from ever arriving — an interruption stream Cole cannot damp and a badge he will learn to
> ignore in week two. Break-even was my whole argument for a 4. It does not survive verification."

### Does it put money in his pocket?

**No, and it currently has no mechanism by which it could.**

The path is: someone decides to talk to Cole → Lead → Client → Engagement → Invoice → Payment.
**Steps 2–6 are built and proven over real HTTP** — integer cents, Invoice and Payment as separate
one-to-many entities, `recordPayment` recomputing paid state from a transactional SUM,
`overdue` derived at read time. That is the best-engineered money model any chair had seen in a
personal system.

**Step 1 does not exist in code.** No inbound capture of any kind. `LEAD_SOURCES` offers
`"instagram"` and `"website"` and nothing in the repo can produce either. Every caller of
`addLead` is Cole typing. Gmail carries `readonly + compose` and **no send scope**.
`outreach.send` and `email.send` are declared outward classes with **no registered finalizer** —
even Cole tapping Confirm returns `no finalizer registered`. The one outward lane that executes,
Instagram, **cannot accept an image from any surface Cole touches**: `hostBytes` (media/host.ts:149)
has zero callers and `imageUrl` appears nowhere in frontend source.

And the offer drafter is aimed at the wrong business — see §2.

> **Revenue Operator:** "The codebase is a beautifully instrumented cash register in a shop with
> no door."

> **Chief of Staff:** "Aurelius can record a dollar. It cannot cause one."

### Why 3 and not 5, and why 3 and not 1

Not 5, because the four things a second operator must do — originate work, act on it, send it,
and be worth more time than it consumes — it does one of, in two narrow classes, both broken.

Not 1, because the hard parts are correct and six adversarial chairs failed to falsify them: the
two-lock outward gate, the confirm whitelist, the tier-aware boot reaper, real inverses, the
money model, deterministic-first rituals, the business-fact revision mechanism. And **every
defect on this council's combined list is between twenty minutes and one day of work.** This is a
right spine with a flooded deck — the recoverable failure.

> **Product Skeptic:** "Four days of work moves it to a 6. Nothing on this list requires a rebuild."

## 2. WHAT IS ACTUALLY TRUE

Findings every chair either produced independently or verified against another chair's citation.

### 2.1 The acting primitive is genuinely correct and outward-safe by construction

Nobody falsified it. Six chairs tried.

- `checkGrantable` (`actionClasses.ts:150-187`) refuses `autonomy.*` by key prefix *before* any
  registry lookup, refuses unknown keys, refuses `tier: "outward"`, refuses training/health domains.
- `isActionGranted` (`grants.ts:83-91`) **re-runs the same check** before reading the row — so a
  hand-written or migrated DB row for an outward class is inert, not dangerous. Two independent
  locks on the same key.
- `confirmAction` claims on a **whitelist** (`status in ['pending','surfaced']`,
  `executor.ts:233`), so a dismissed or undone proposal cannot be resurrected by a stale tab.
  Dismiss and Undo mean *no*, permanently.
- `reapStaleActing` (`executor.ts:153-196`) releases stranded **inward** rows to pending but
  escalates **outward** rows — and any *unknown* class — to `surfaced` with a critical warning
  rather than re-arming, on the reasoning that a crash may land after the outward API call
  succeeded. The unknown case defaults to the expensive-but-safe branch.
- Undo is real, not a label: `registerActions.ts:23-26` deletes the actual Google event the
  finalizer created.

### 2.2 The money model is correct and is the only capability proven end-to-end over real HTTP

`crm/service.ts` — integer cents; Invoice (owed) and Payment (received) separate and one-to-many;
`recordPayment` transactional, recomputing paid state from `prisma.payment.aggregate` SUM rather
than incrementing; `overdue` derived at read time, never stored; three billing shapes with
distinct date semantics (block gets `endsAt` that *is* the re-sign conversation, monthly gets
`nextBillingAt` with no end, program gets neither); MRR excludes blocks and programs. ~25 smoke
assertions including the two cases most CRMs get wrong — partial payment leaving the correct
remainder, and MRR shape exclusion.

### 2.3 The system refuses to flatter an empty pipeline — on one surface

`crm/service.ts:568-578` headline: *"The remote pipeline is empty — no clients, no open leads.
The constraint is lead generation, not tracking: this roster can't help until there's someone in
it."* Smoke-enforced. The `/business` empty state (`business/page.tsx:170-184`) is, in the Product
Skeptic's words, "the most honest empty state I have audited."

It reaches a web page. It reaches nothing else. See §3.4.

### 2.4 Deterministic-first rituals are the reason the system degrades gracefully

`rituals/engine.ts:42-67` — `voiceOver` returns the deterministic skeleton when the model is
unavailable; every confronting footer (risk line, tomorrow-watch, debrief→dawn thread) is computed
from Postgres and appended *after* the voice pass, so a word budget cannot compress it away.
`fileInstance()` persists the full briefing before the Telegram push, so a failed push does not
lose the content. Cole gets a real 07:00 artifact with zero credentials.

### 2.5 The business-fact correction was made at the source, not in prose

`business/profile.ts` — per-fact `revision`, `RETIRED_FACTS` soft-deactivation, hand-edited
entries (`sourceId` ≠ `profile:rev{N}`) never overwritten, reconciled into Living Knowledge at
every boot (`index.ts:1765-1775`). Four chairs named this the single best design added in the
last week. A correction reaches the database on deploy rather than waiting for someone to

## APPENDIX — THE ONE STRUCTURAL FINDING

The Systems Architect's revised sharpest point, because it names why the same defect keeps
appearing under different names:

> "The council found six defects today and called them six different things. They are one thing,

   tier prices to null.

Rule 8's audit cannot see any of these, because in all five the invoker **exists** and is simply
not the declared one. Until the repo's checks assert **exclusivity** rather than existence,
feature eleven adds a sixth.
